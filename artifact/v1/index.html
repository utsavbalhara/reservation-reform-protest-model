Agent-based simulation · 146 crore population · 50 Monte Carlo runs per scenario

Reservation Reform Protest Model

If India replaced caste-based reservation with an income-only ₹8 lakh test, how could protest be kept small? I tested nine interventions and two packages against a calibrated baseline of about 46 lakh people on the peak day.

Scope: this tests policy design, sequencing and coalition-building. Suppression tactics are included only to measure whether they backfire. The numbers are illustrative; the ranking of interventions is the robust output.

What the simulation says

  • −94%Keeping caste as the qualifier and adding an income filter inside each quota is the single strongest lever. Most of the anger in this reform is about losing caste recognition, not about losing a seat. It stays above −80% even if the hybrid defuses only a fifth of that symbolic threat.
  • −82%A data-first commission with cross-party buy-in is the strongest lever that keeps the full income-only end state. It works by keeping opposition parties from supplying organization. Dropping it from the managed package triples turnout.
  • −95%A managed transition (grandfathering, seat expansion, consensus, compensation, guarantees) reaches the same income-only end state with about 2 lakh on the peak day.
  • ×3–4Internet shutdowns and heavy policing lower peak turnout only 7–29%, raise total participation by 9–25%, and triple or quadruple deaths. This matches India's shutdown record.

Why these levers, and why they rank this way

Loss aversion

Losses weigh about twice as much as equal gains. Retrenchment is harder than expansion because costs are concentrated and immediate while gains are diffuse. Grandfathering and glide paths shrink the loss people feel today.

Symbolic threat

2018 and 2024 bandhs followed changes that removed nobody's eligibility. For this reform, threat to caste recognition drives more turnout than material loss, which is why compensation alone does little.

Threshold cascades

People join when enough of their neighbours already have. Turnout responds non-linearly: a lever that lowers grievance below the tipping point removes far more protest than its direct effect suggests.

Elite cues and organization

Party machinery turns grievance into buses, bandh calls and crowds. When opposition parties share ownership of a reform, that capacity is withheld.

The EWS quota in 2019 is the domestic proof of concept for seat expansion. The government added about 2.14 lakh seats in central institutions, a roughly 25% intake increase, so that no existing category lost seats, and the change passed without mass street protest. Brazil's 2012 Quota Law is the closest foreign analogue to the hybrid: race-based sub-quotas sit inside a public-school and income filter, which in effect creates a creamy layer while keeping racial recognition.

The model

  1. Population. 120,000 weighted agents (each about 12,000 people) with shares of SC 16.6%, ST 8.6%, OBC 42%, General 32.8%. About 20% sit above the ₹8L line, concentrated in General and the better-off SC/ST sub-castes. SC/ST are split into a better-off tier and a most-deprived tier.
  2. Grievance. Loss-averse material loss (λ ≈ 2.25) plus symbolic threat scaled by each agent's identity strength. SC/ST above the line lose eligibility; those below lose from quota pools merging; poor General-category families gain slightly.
  3. Participation. Each day an agent protests if grievance, plus saturating influence from its 100-person local cluster and its group's national turnout, plus organizational mobilization on bandh days, exceeds its personal threshold. Thresholds are wide-tailed to include low-threshold activists. Participation raises the agent's threshold (fatigue).
  4. Violence. Deaths arrive in proportion to turnout. Each death raises symbolic threat for the groups involved (the martyr effect). Shutdowns cut coordination by 40% and multiply violence by 2.5, following Rydzak's India findings. Heavy policing raises turnout costs on bandh days but triples the death rate.
  5. Calibration. Mean threshold is set so the baseline gives about 46 lakh peak, 1.3 crore cumulative and 18 deaths, matching the earlier estimate. As a validation check, a symbolic-only shock with no material loss gives about 13 lakh, the scale of the 2018 bandh.
  6. Uncertainty. Each run draws loss aversion (±30%), symbolic weights (±20%), party amplification, social-influence strengths, mean threshold and violence rate. All scenarios share the same draws per run, so comparisons are paired.

Limits: grievance weights, lever effects and cluster structure are assumptions set from qualitative evidence, not estimated from Indian protest data. Regional variation, counter-protests, courts and elections are not modelled. Treat magnitudes as order-of-magnitude and the ranking as the finding.
